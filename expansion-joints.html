Expansion Joint Treatment

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Joints allow structures to move with temperature, shrinkage and loading. Joint treatment seals them against water and debris while still allowing that movement.

Important: General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Building expansion joints
  • Industrial and warehouse floors
  • Roofs and terraces
  • Basements
  • Construction joints

Common problems addressed

  • Water leakage at joints
  • Failed or hardened sealants
  • Debris in joints
  • Edge damage in floor joints

Applications & scope

Building expansion joints

Joints separating structural blocks.

Floor joints

Joints in slabs and industrial floors.

Roof & terrace joints

Weather-exposed joints.

Basement joints

Joints exposed to water pressure.

Construction joints

Joints between concrete pours.

Movement joint sealants

Selected for movement capability.

Backer rods & joint preparation

Control sealant depth and prevent three-sided bonding.

Flexible joint waterproofing systems

Membranes or tapes bridging joints.

Suitable system options

  • PU sealants
  • Polysulphide sealants (where available)
  • Backer rods and bond breakers
  • Primers
  • Joint waterproofing tapes and membranes

Application methodology

Joint Survey→Old Sealant Removal→Joint Cleaning→Backer Rod→Primer→Sealant Application→Tooling→Inspection
  1. Measure joint width, depth and expected movement.
  2. Remove failed sealant and loose edges.
  3. Clean and dry joint faces.
  4. Install backer rod at the correct depth.
  5. Apply primer where the datasheet requires it.
  6. Fill with sealant, avoiding air voids.
  7. Tool the surface and remove masking.
  8. Cure and inspect.

Surface preparation requirements

  • Sound joint edges
  • Dust, oil and old sealant removed
  • Dry joint faces
  • Backer rod of suitable size

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • Sealants
  • Backer rods
  • Primers
  • Masking tape
  • Joint waterproofing membranes

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Joint dimensions checked
  • Backer rod depth set
  • Primer coverage
  • Sealant fully fills the joint
  • No air bubbles; cured surface checked

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Wrong sealant for movement
  • No backer rod, causing three-sided adhesion
  • Poor joint cleaning
  • Incompatible primer/sealant
  • Wrong width-to-depth ratio

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Joint length and dimensions
  • Sealant type
  • Removal of old material
  • Access
  • Exposure conditions

No prices or consumption figures are published here; they depend on the site and the product datasheet.

Frequently asked questions

Why is joint width-to-depth ratio important?
It affects how much the sealant can stretch without tearing. Follow the manufacturer's recommended ratio.
Can any sealant be used in a joint?
No. Select according to movement, joint dimensions, exposure and substrate.
