PU & Epoxy Injection Grouting

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Injection grouting forces specially selected resins into cracks, joints or voids. Polyurethane (PU) injection is mainly used to stop water, while epoxy injection is used to bond suitable cracks.

Important: Not every crack is suitable for injection. Resin selection depends on the crack, moisture, movement and structural requirements. General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Basements and retaining walls
  • Tunnels, shafts and underground structures
  • Water tanks and sumps
  • Cracked slabs, beams and walls
  • Construction joints and cold joints

Common problems addressed

  • Active water leaks through cracks and joints
  • Seepage in basements
  • Cracks needing bonding
  • Voids behind structures

Applications & scope

PU injection

For active leakage and water-bearing cracks; resin type chosen by moisture and leak conditions.

Epoxy injection

For suitable cracks needing bonding, subject to engineering assessment.

Void / gap filling

Grouting voids as specified.

Suitable system options

  • Hydrophobic / hydrophilic PU injection resins (as suited to conditions)
  • Low-viscosity epoxy injection resins
  • Surface-mounted or drilled packers
  • Crack sealing pastes

Application methodology

Inspection→Crack Preparation→Packer Installation→Resin Mixing→Controlled Injection→Monitoring→Finishing & Inspection
  1. Inspect the crack or joint and decide whether injection is suitable.
  2. Prepare the crack: clean and seal the surface where needed.
  3. Install injection packers at the specified spacing.
  4. Mix resin according to the product datasheet (for epoxy, observe ratios and pot life).
  5. Inject in a controlled sequence and at controlled pressure.
  6. Monitor for leaks or resin travel; reinject if required.
  7. Cure, remove packers, finish the surface and inspect.

Surface preparation requirements

  • Crack identified and mapped
  • Surface cleaned of dust and loose material
  • Packer locations marked
  • Safe access arranged

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • PU injection resins and accelerators
  • Epoxy injection resins
  • Packers, injection pumps
  • Sealing putty or mortar

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Resin and batch recorded
  • Mixing ratios and pot life followed
  • Pressure controlled
  • Leak-free condition verified
  • Finished surface inspected

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Injecting epoxy into actively leaking cracks
  • Wrong resin for moisture condition
  • Injecting too fast or at excessive pressure
  • Treating moving cracks as static
  • Not reinjecting where needed

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Crack length and width
  • Resin type
  • Number of packers
  • Access and working conditions
  • Number of reinjection cycles

No prices or consumption figures are published here; they depend on the site and the product datasheet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between PU and epoxy injection?
PU is generally used to stop water, including active leaks. Epoxy is generally used where the crack needs bonding in dry or suitable conditions. Selection depends on the crack, moisture, movement and structural requirements.
Is every crack suitable for injection?
No. Moving cracks, very wide cracks and structurally significant cracks need assessment before choosing a method.
