Epoxy & PU Flooring Solutions

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Resin flooring creates seamless, durable surfaces on concrete floors. Epoxy and polyurethane systems are used where abrasion, hygiene, chemical exposure or appearance matters.

Important: Floor system suitability depends on loads, chemicals and temperature. General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Factories and warehouses
  • Pharmaceutical and food areas
  • Parking and utility areas
  • Commercial spaces and showrooms
  • Workshops and laboratories

Common problems addressed

  • Dusting and surface wear
  • Chemical attack on concrete
  • Hygiene and cleanability
  • Cracked or damaged floors
  • Slippery surfaces

Applications & scope

Industrial epoxy flooring

Coatings or screeds for general industrial use.

Self-leveling epoxy flooring

Smooth seamless finishes over prepared substrates.

Heavy-duty industrial floors

Thicker systems for traffic and abrasion.

PU flooring systems

Flexible, abrasion-resistant systems for suitable applications.

Anti-slip flooring

Textured finishes where slip resistance is required.

Chemical-resistant flooring

Systems matched to the specific chemicals present.

Warehouse & factory flooring

Dust-free, easy-to-clean floors.

Commercial & utility flooring

Decorative and functional floors.

Suitable system options

  • Epoxy coatings and screeds
  • Self-leveling epoxy
  • PU topcoats and PU screeds
  • Anti-slip aggregates
  • Chemical-resistant epoxy / novolac-type systems as specified

Application methodology

Substrate Assessment→Moisture Test→Mechanical Grinding→Primer→Base Coat→Aggregate / Intermediate Layer (if required)→Topcoat→Curing→Final Inspection
  1. Assess the substrate strength, cracks and joints.
  2. Assess moisture content and the potential for moisture vapour transmission.
  3. Mechanically grind or shot-blast to a sound, open-textured surface; vacuum dust.
  4. Repair cracks, holes and joints.
  5. Apply primer as specified.
  6. Apply base coat or screed.
  7. Broadcast aggregate or apply intermediate layer where required.
  8. Apply topcoat; observe recoat windows.
  9. Allow curing before traffic and chemical exposure; inspect.

Surface preparation requirements

  • Concrete of adequate strength and age
  • Moisture within the product's limits
  • Laitance, oil and contaminants removed
  • Dust-free surface
  • Controlled temperature and humidity during application

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • Primers
  • Epoxy / PU base and top coats
  • Aggregates and fillers
  • Joint sealants
  • Cleaning and repair materials

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Substrate moisture recorded
  • Ambient and substrate temperature checked
  • Mixing ratios followed
  • Thickness checked
  • Cure times respected
  • Final visual inspection

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping moisture testing
  • Inadequate grinding
  • Applying in unsuitable temperature or humidity
  • Ignoring joints and movement
  • Using a system not suited to the chemical exposure

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Floor area and thickness
  • Preparation required
  • System type
  • Anti-slip or chemical resistance needs
  • Working hours and site access

No prices or consumption figures are published here; they depend on the site and the product datasheet.

Frequently asked questions

Can epoxy be applied over any concrete floor?
Only if the substrate is sound, suitably dry and properly prepared. Moisture and strength must be assessed.
Epoxy or PU?
The right choice depends on traffic, chemicals, flexibility, temperature and finish requirements.
