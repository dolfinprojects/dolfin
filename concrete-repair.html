Concrete Repair & Restoration

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Concrete repair restores damaged or deteriorated concrete so it can perform as intended. It covers removing unsound material, treating exposed reinforcement, and rebuilding the section with compatible repair materials.

Important: Repairs that affect structural capacity should be assessed by a qualified structural engineer. General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Beams, columns, slabs and balconies
  • Industrial and commercial buildings
  • Bridges, culverts and infrastructure
  • Parking structures and podiums
  • Water-retaining and marine-exposed structures
  • RMC plants and process facilities

Common problems addressed

  • Cracking and delamination
  • Exposed or rusting reinforcement
  • Honeycombing and voids
  • Edge damage and spalling
  • Chemical or environmental deterioration
  • Loss of cover to reinforcement

Applications & scope

Honeycomb concrete repair

Cutting back and refilling poorly compacted zones.

Spalling & damaged concrete repair

Removing delaminated concrete and reinstating the profile.

Crack filling & treatment

Routing and sealing, or injection, depending on the crack.

Corrosion-related repair

Treating corroded reinforcement and restoring cover.

Patch repair

Local repairs with compatible mortars.

Polymer-modified repair mortar

Hand or trowel-applied repair for general to moderate-depth repairs.

Flowable micro-concrete

Poured or pumped repair into formwork for deeper or confined sections.

Structural & non-structural repair materials

Matching material class to the repair requirement and engineer's specification.

Bonding agents & repair primers

Improving adhesion between substrate and repair layer where specified.

Suitable system options

  • Cementitious repair mortars
  • Polymer-modified repair mortars
  • Flowable micro-concrete
  • Epoxy or cementitious bonding systems
  • Reinforcement protection coatings, as specified
  • Surface protective coatings after repair

Application methodology

Condition Assessment→Removal of Unsound Concrete→Reinforcement Inspection & Treatment→Surface Preparation→Bonding System (if specified)→Repair Mortar / Micro-concrete→Curing→Quality Inspection
  1. Assess the condition: sound the concrete, mark delaminated zones and review the cause of damage.
  2. Remove unsound concrete to sound substrate, with clean, defined edges.
  3. Inspect reinforcement; remove rust and treat or protect it as specified. Escalate to a structural engineer if section loss is significant.
  4. Prepare and clean the surface; dampen if the datasheet requires.
  5. Apply a bonding system if specified.
  6. Place repair mortar or micro-concrete in layers or formwork as per datasheet.
  7. Cure properly to avoid shrinkage cracking.
  8. Inspect the finished repair and apply protective coating if specified.

Surface preparation requirements

  • All loose, delaminated and contaminated concrete removed
  • Rust removed from reinforcement to the specified standard
  • Clean, roughened, saturated-surface-dry substrate where the datasheet requires
  • Edges cut to avoid feather edges

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • Repair mortars and micro-concrete
  • Bonding agents and primers
  • Reinforcement protection products
  • Curing compounds
  • Protective coatings

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Extent of removal verified
  • Reinforcement condition recorded
  • Mixing water and mixing time as per datasheet
  • Layer thickness within product limits
  • Curing method applied and maintained
  • Sound/tap test after hardening

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving unsound concrete behind
  • Ignoring the cause of corrosion or damage
  • Over-thick single layers
  • Using a non-compatible repair material
  • Poor or no curing
  • Treating structural damage as cosmetic

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Quantity and depth of repair
  • Access and scaffolding
  • Reinforcement treatment needed
  • Material class (structural / non-structural)
  • Formwork needs for micro-concrete
  • Protective coating after repair

No prices or consumption figures are published here; they depend on the site and the product datasheet.

Frequently asked questions

Is every crack repaired the same way?
No. Crack width, movement, cause and whether it is structural all influence the method. Some cracks need engineering assessment.
Why is the reinforcement important?
Corroded steel expands and cracks the surrounding concrete. Repairs that ignore it often fail again.
Do you supply repair mortars?
