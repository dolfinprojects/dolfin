Waterproofing Systems & Application Services

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Waterproofing is the selection and professional application of materials that stop water entering, or moving through, a structure. Leakage, dampness and seepage usually have a specific cause — a failed membrane, an unsealed joint, a crack, poor drainage or hydrostatic pressure — so the right fix starts with diagnosis, not just a coating.

Important: Final system must depend on site conditions, substrate, water pressure, movement and the manufacturer's specification. General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Terraces and roofs
  • Basements and retaining walls
  • Overhead and underground water tanks
  • Toilets, bathrooms and other wet areas
  • Podiums, planters and balconies
  • Swimming pools and utility structures

Common problems addressed

  • Roof or slab leakage and ceiling stains
  • Rising dampness and seepage through walls
  • Cracks that admit water
  • Failed or aged waterproofing layers
  • Leakage at joints, pipe penetrations and junctions
  • Efflorescence, peeling paint and mould linked to moisture

Applications & scope

Terrace & roof waterproofing

Surface-applied membranes or coatings with proper slope, drainage, terminations and protective screed.

Basement waterproofing

Positive-side or negative-side systems chosen according to water table and access.

Retaining wall waterproofing

Membranes and drainage layers on the earth-retaining face, protected from backfill.

Water tank waterproofing

Systems suitable for the stored-water contact conditions — always confirm product suitability for the intended use.

Toilet & bathroom waterproofing

Wet-area systems with detailing at pipe penetrations, wall-floor junctions and drains, with flood testing.

Podium & balcony waterproofing

Systems that suit movement, traffic and finishes laid above.

Crack & leakage treatment

Crack routing, sealing or injection before any surface system.

PU injection waterproofing

Resin injection into active leaks and water-bearing cracks.

Cementitious waterproofing

Rigid or polymer-modified cementitious coatings for suitable, low-movement substrates.

Liquid-applied membranes

Seamless membranes with reinforcement at details, selected for movement and exposure.

Crystalline waterproofing

Crystalline products that work within the concrete pore structure, per datasheet.

Suitable system options

  • Cementitious (rigid / polymer-modified) coatings
  • Acrylic and polymer-modified coatings
  • Liquid-applied membranes (e.g. PU or similar, as specified)
  • Crystalline systems
  • PU / acrylate injection for active leaks
  • Integral waterproofing admixtures in new concrete
  • Joint waterproofing materials and sealants

Application methodology

Inspection→Diagnosis→Surface Preparation→Primer (if specified)→Waterproofing Application→Detailing & Reinforcement (where required)→Curing / Drying→Testing→Handover
  1. Inspect the area, trace the source of water and record moisture, cracks, slope and drainage.
  2. Diagnose the cause and shortlist suitable systems for the substrate, water pressure and movement.
  3. Prepare the surface: remove loose material, repair cracks and honeycombing, clean and make the substrate sound.
  4. Apply primer where the datasheet requires it.
  5. Apply the waterproofing system in the specified coats or layers, observing thickness and overlap/recoat intervals.
  6. Detail junctions, corners, pipe penetrations and joints, using reinforcement where required.
  7. Allow curing or drying as specified before loading or covering.
  8. Test — typically a ponding or flood test where practicable — and inspect for leaks.
  9. Hand over with a record of the system applied and care instructions.

Surface preparation requirements

  • Sound, clean substrate free from dust, oil, laitance and loose particles
  • Cracks, honeycombs and cavities repaired first
  • Coving or fillets at wall–floor junctions where specified
  • Correct slope to drains; drains and pipe sleeves fixed before application
  • Moisture condition suited to the chosen product

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • Cementitious and polymer-modified coatings
  • Liquid membranes and primers
  • Crystalline products
  • PU / acrylate injection resins
  • Sealants, backer rods, reinforcing fabrics
  • Protective screed or tiles over membranes

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Substrate inspected and accepted before application
  • Primer and coat intervals as per datasheet
  • Wet film / consumption checked against specification
  • Details (junctions, penetrations) inspected
  • Curing observed; ponding or flood test where practicable
  • Documented handover

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying over dusty, weak or damp-unsuitable substrate
  • Skipping detailing at corners, pipes and junctions
  • Choosing a rigid system where movement is expected
  • Insufficient thickness or missed coats
  • Covering with screed or tiles before curing
  • Ignoring drainage and slope

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Area and number of details/penetrations
  • System selected and required thickness
  • Substrate condition and repairs needed
  • Access, height and working conditions
  • Testing and protection layers required
  • Product consumption per datasheet

Frequently asked questions

Which waterproofing system is best for my terrace?
It depends on the existing surface, movement, expected finish and exposure. A site inspection lets us recommend suitable options rather than a one-size answer.
Can leakage be fixed without breaking the tiles?
Sometimes, for example with injection or surface treatments, but it depends on where the water is entering. Diagnosis comes first.
Do you provide the products as well as the application?
DOLFIN PROJECTS offers both product supply and application services. Scope and terms are confirmed in the quotation.
How long does waterproofing take?
It varies with area, system, drying/curing times and weather, so timelines are confirmed after inspection.
