Structural Rehabilitation & Strengthening

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Structural rehabilitation restores or improves the performance of ageing, damaged or under-capacity structures. It combines condition assessment, repair, protection and, where designed, strengthening.

Important: Structural strengthening must be designed or approved by a qualified structural engineer. Nothing on this website is a structural design recommendation. General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Older residential and commercial buildings
  • Industrial structures and factories
  • Bridges and infrastructure
  • Parking structures
  • Buildings with changed use or added loads

Common problems addressed

  • Cracks of structural significance
  • Corrosion damage and section loss
  • Reduced load capacity
  • Deterioration from environment or chemicals
  • Design changes needing additional capacity

Applications & scope

Structural condition assessment

Visual inspection and testing, coordinated with a qualified structural engineer.

Concrete deterioration repair

Removal and replacement of deteriorated concrete.

Reinforcement protection

Treatment and protection of corroded steel.

Structural grouting

Filling voids and gaps to restore load transfer, as specified.

Crack injection

Epoxy injection where assessed as suitable.

Carbon fibre strengthening systems

Externally bonded systems installed to an engineered design.

Jacketing & strengthening solutions

Section enlargement and similar methods as designed.

Load-bearing element rehabilitation

Columns, beams, slabs and foundations — only per engineer's scheme.

Suitable system options

  • Structural repair mortars and micro-concrete
  • Epoxy injection resins
  • Non-shrink and structural grouts
  • Externally bonded carbon fibre systems (as designed)
  • Reinforcement protection systems
  • Protective coatings

Application methodology

Structural Assessment→Engineer's Design / Approval→Surface & Reinforcement Preparation→Repair / Strengthening Execution→Quality Control→Protection→Handover
  1. Arrange structural condition assessment through a qualified structural engineer.
  2. Obtain the engineer's repair or strengthening scheme and material approvals.
  3. Prepare the structure: remove unsound concrete, treat reinforcement, prepare surfaces.
  4. Execute repair, grouting, injection or strengthening per the approved scheme.
  5. Carry out inspections and tests required by the specification.
  6. Apply protective systems.
  7. Hand over documentation.

Surface preparation requirements

  • Engineer-approved scope before work starts
  • Temporary support where required by the engineer
  • Surface preparation per the specific system
  • Records of existing condition

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • Structural repair materials
  • Epoxy and cementitious grouts
  • Injection resins
  • Carbon fibre systems (where designed)
  • Protective coatings

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Work follows the approved scheme
  • Material batch and mixing records
  • Bond and cure checks as specified
  • Engineer inspections at hold points

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Strengthening without an engineer's design
  • Treating symptoms and not causes
  • Using unapproved materials
  • Skipping curing and inspections

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Scope set by the engineer's design
  • Extent of deterioration
  • Access and temporary works
  • Material systems specified
  • Testing and documentation

No prices or consumption figures are published here; they depend on the site and the product datasheet.

Frequently asked questions

Can DOLFIN PROJECTS design the strengthening?
Structural strengthening must be designed or approved by a qualified structural engineer. We support execution and material supply to that scheme.
Is carbon fibre always the answer?
No. The method is chosen by the engineer based on the structure, loads and condition.
