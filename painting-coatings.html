Industrial Painting & Protective Coatings

Professional application with technical guidance, from inspection to handover.

What it is

Protective coatings shield concrete and steel from moisture, chemicals, abrasion and corrosion. The correct system depends on the substrate and the exposure.

Important: Do not assume one coating suits every chemical exposure or water-contact application. General information only. Final system selection depends on site inspection, substrate condition, exposure, movement and the manufacturer's technical datasheet.

Where it is used

  • Industrial plants and process areas
  • Water tanks and utility structures
  • STP and ETP structures
  • Steel structures and supports
  • Concrete floors and walls

Common problems addressed

  • Corrosion of steel
  • Chemical attack on concrete
  • Abrasion and wear
  • Dampness and surface deterioration

Application methodology

Inspection→Substrate Preparation→Primer Selection & Application→Intermediate / Build Coats→Topcoat→Curing→Inspection
  1. Inspect the structure and define the exposure conditions.
  2. Prepare the substrate: clean, abrade or blast, and repair defects.
  3. Select and apply a compatible primer.
  4. Apply the coats to specified dry film thickness, observing recoat times.
  5. Apply topcoat.
  6. Cure as specified.
  7. Inspect thickness, continuity and appearance.

Surface preparation requirements

  • Clean, dry, sound substrate
  • Correct surface profile
  • Rust removed on steel to specified grade
  • Compatibility of primer and coating confirmed

Typical materials & chemical categories

  • Primers
  • Epoxy and PU coatings
  • Fillers and surface repair materials
  • Thinners and cleaning solvents per datasheet

Quality-control checkpoints

  • Surface preparation inspected
  • Ambient conditions measured
  • Thickness checks
  • Coat-to-coat intervals
  • Final inspection

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming one coating suits every chemical
  • Inadequate surface preparation
  • Wrong primer
  • Exceeding recoat windows
  • Applying in damp or hot conditions

Factors affecting cost and consumption

  • Surface area
  • Substrate and preparation
  • Number of coats and thickness
  • Exposure severity
  • Access and shutdown windows

No prices or consumption figures are published here; they depend on the site and the product datasheet.

Frequently asked questions

Can one coating be used everywhere?
No. Chemical exposure, water contact, temperature and substrate all affect the choice.
Are all coatings safe for drinking-water tanks?
No. Only use products confirmed suitable for that purpose by the manufacturer.
